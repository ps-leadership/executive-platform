# Free Work Is Not Free: The Hidden Currency of Collaboration

In most organizations, collaboration extends beyond what is formally planned or purchased.

A customer may ask for help that sits outside an agreed engagement. A team may step in because the issue is urgent, the relationship matters, or saying no feels more expensive than helping.

That flexibility can be valuable. The problem begins when exceptional effort quietly becomes the operating model.

## When a favor becomes an expectation

What starts as an occasional request can become normalized.

Specialists are repeatedly pulled into unplanned work. Priorities shift without being reset. The same reliable people absorb the effort because they are trusted to get things done.

The financial cost may never appear as a line item. Instead, it shows up as reduced capacity for strategic work, fatigue, slower execution, and less attention for the priorities that were actually planned.

The work is happening. It is simply happening outside the system's visibility.

## The real issue is governance

Organizations often manage this kind of work through goodwill:

> "Let's help this time."

> "We'll sort it out later."

> "The relationship is important."

Those decisions can be completely reasonable. But when every exception is handled informally, leadership loses visibility into the cumulative investment.

The question is no longer whether people should help. It is whether the organization is making a conscious choice about where that help is being invested.

## Treat effort as a value currency

A useful shift is to recognize out-of-scope effort as a limited organizational resource.

That does not mean putting a price tag on every conversation. It means acknowledging that specialist attention, engineering capacity, leadership time, and extended coverage are finite.

Once that effort becomes visible, leaders can make better choices:

- continue the investment because the relationship or outcome justifies it;
- change the scope or engagement model;
- redirect scarce expertise;
- or establish a clearer boundary for future requests.

The goal is not to reduce collaboration. It is to make collaboration intentional.

## Why visibility matters

For customers, clearer boundaries reduce surprises.

For teams, visible effort signals that their contribution is recognized rather than silently absorbed.

For leaders, it creates a better view of where capacity is going and where the operating model may need to change.

Goodwill remains valuable. But goodwill should support the operating model, not become the operating model.

## Closing thought

Every strong organization depends on people who are willing to step beyond what is written.

That willingness becomes a problem only when it is assumed, repeated, and invisible.

**Free work is not free. It is simply unaccounted for.**

And what remains unaccounted for cannot be managed.
